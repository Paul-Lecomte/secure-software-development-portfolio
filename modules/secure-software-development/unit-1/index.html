Assignment: IoT Healthcare — Misuse Case Analysis

Initial Post — Misuse Case Analysis for IoT Healthcare Systems

Introduction

I have selected IoT healthcare as the domain for this analysis. Healthcare Internet of Things (IoT) systems connect devices such as wearable sensors, remote patient-monitoring equipment, smart infusion pumps and hospital information systems. These technologies can support continuous monitoring, improve access to care and help clinicians make timely decisions. However, connecting medical devices and patient records also creates security risks. Li et al. (2024) review healthcare IoT applications and identify data security and interoperability as important challenges.

Misuse case analysis

The misuse case diagram identifies legitimate system functions and ways malicious actors could compromise them. The main actors are patients, healthcare professionals, external attackers and malicious insiders. Legitimate use cases include monitoring vital signs, viewing patient records, transmitting readings and managing medical devices. The misuse cases include credential theft, interception or alteration of medical data, ransomware and unauthorised access to patient records. Misuse cases are useful during requirements analysis because they connect harmful actions to the system functions they threaten (Sindre and Opdahl, 2005).

These threats affect more than confidentiality. Unauthorised disclosure can violate patient privacy, while altered readings or disrupted monitoring can undermine clinical decisions. The actual impact depends on the device, clinical context and availability of alternative procedures. Healthcare IoT security must therefore protect confidentiality, integrity and availability while taking patient safety into account (Kashani et al., 2021; Karunarathne, Saxena and Khan, 2021).

Rationale and risk reduction

The diagram highlights where controls should be introduced during system design rather than only after an incident. Suggested controls include multi-factor authentication, role-based access control, encryption in transit and at rest, network segmentation, secure device updates, audit logging and tested backup and recovery procedures. Security and privacy need to be considered across device manufacture, communication, data handling, storage and end-of-life processes, rather than at a single point in the system (Karunarathne, Saxena and Khan, 2021).

No single control addresses every threat. Encryption can reduce the risk of intercepted data being read, but it will not stop an authorised account from being misused or guarantee that a compromised sensor produces accurate readings. Network segmentation can limit ransomware spread, but it must be combined with patch management, monitoring and tested recovery plans. Reviews of healthcare IoT also highlight the need to address security challenges alongside interoperability and practical deployment constraints (Li et al., 2024; Selvaraj and Sundaravaradhan, 2020).

Conclusion

Misuse case modelling provides a practical way to connect attackers, harmful actions, affected healthcare functions and possible controls. In IoT healthcare, this is especially important because a cyber incident may affect both information security and patient care. The diagram should be reviewed as devices, software, clinical workflows and threats change.
